A wig lasts longer with a little care often than a lot of care now and then. This is the routine for a synthetic wig worn most days.

<p><button class="button print-btn" onclick="window.print()">Print this routine</button></p>

## Every time you take it off

- Shake it gently
- Comb out any tangles with a wide-tooth comb, starting at the ends
- Put it on a stand, away from radiators and sunny windowsills
{: .checklist }

## Every six to eight wears

Jon Renau recommends washing about this often. Don't stretch it to make the wig last. Oil and product that build up damage the hair and the cap.

- Wash in cool water with wig shampoo
- Rinse, then blot with a towel. Don't wring or rub
- Dry on a stand overnight. No hairdryer
- Shake it back into shape once it's dry
{: .checklist }

Full steps: [how to wash a synthetic wig](/blogs/guides/how-to-wash-a-synthetic-wig).

## Once a month

- Check the nape for frizz. See [fixing frizz and tangles](/blogs/guides/fixing-frizz-tangles-and-shine-on-synthetic-wigs)
- Check the adjusters and ear tabs still hold
- Look inside the cap for loose stitching
- Wash your wig cap or grip
{: .checklist }

## Keep it away from

- Oven doors, barbecues and kettle steam
- Hair straighteners and tongs, unless the label says heat-friendly
- Ordinary hairspray and shampoo
{: .checklist }

## When to think about a new one

Worn every day, a synthetic wig usually lasts some months, not years. Macmillan suggests four to eight. It's time when the ends stay frizzy after washing, the style no longer holds, or the cap has stretched. See [how long do wigs last?](/blogs/guides/how-long-do-wigs-last)
