The first time you put a wig on, it'll probably sit too far forward, and you'll wonder why it looks wrong. Almost everyone does this. It takes two or three goes to get the knack, and then it takes ten seconds.

## Before you start

**If you have hair,** flatten it. Short hair can be brushed back. Longer hair is best pinned flat or wrapped round your head and tucked under a wig cap.

**If you have little or no hair,** you can put the wig straight on, or wear a thin liner first for comfort.

Give the wig a gentle shake to loosen the fibres, and check the adjusters at the back are let out a little. It's easier to tighten once it's on.

## Putting it on

1. **Hold it at the nape** with both hands, the label at the back.
2. **Tip your head forward a little** and place the front of the wig at your forehead.
3. **Draw it back and down over your head**, the way you'd pull on a swimming cap.
4. **Find the ear tabs.** These are the small flat tabs in front of each ear. Line them up so they sit level, just in front of your ears. Don't pull them down over your ears.
5. **Settle the front.** It should sit where your own hairline is, or was.
6. **Tighten the adjusters** at the nape until it feels snug but not tight.

If your wig has a lace front, handle it by the sides and the ear tabs. The lace along the forehead is delicate and tears if you tug it.

## Is it in the right place?

A wig worn too low is the commonest mistake, and it's what makes a wig look heavy.

- A rough guide many fitters use is four fingers' width above your eyebrows. That's about where most hairlines sit.
- The ear tabs should be even on both sides. If one is higher, the wig is on crooked.
- At the back, the wig should cover your nape hairline without riding up when you look down.

Then do Macmillan's test. Bend forward and shake your head gently. If it stays put, you're done.

## Five ways to keep it secure

**1. The adjusters.** Start here. Most slipping is a wig that's simply a notch too loose.

**2. A wig grip band.** A soft band worn under the wig that stops it sliding. Useful on a smooth scalp and kind to your skin.

**3. Tape.** Macmillan suggests hypoallergenic double-sided tape made for wigs. Small strips at the temples and nape are usually enough. If you're having treatment, check with your doctor or nurse first.

**4. Adhesive pads.** Cancer Research UK mentions these for when all your hair has gone.

**5. Clips or combs.** Some wigs have small combs sewn in that grip your own hair. Only any use if you have hair for them to hold.

You don't need all of these. Most women settle on the adjusters and one other.

## Taking it off

Loosen any tape gently first. Then lift from the nape and bring the wig forward and off. Pulling it off from the front stretches the hairline.

Put it on a stand, not in a heap. It keeps its shape, and it's ready for tomorrow.

## If it still moves

Check the size. A wig that slips however you secure it is usually too big. See [how to measure your head](/blogs/guides/how-to-measure-your-head-for-a-wig). For more on the bits and pieces that help, see [wig caps, grips and tapes](/blogs/guides/wig-caps-grips-and-tapes).
