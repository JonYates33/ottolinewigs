Turn a wig inside out and you're looking at the cap. It's the bit nobody sees and the bit you feel from morning to night, so it deserves more thought than it usually gets.

There are four terms you'll meet again and again. Most wigs combine two or three of them.

## Wefted (also called open cap or basic cap)

Hair is sewn onto strips of material called wefts, and the wefts are stitched into rows with gaps between them. Those gaps let air through.

- **Feels:** light and cool. Alopecia UK's guide describes wefted caps as breathable and comfortable for a sensitive scalp.
- **Looks:** good from the outside, but there's a little built-in lift at the crown to hide the wefts, and you can't change the parting.
- **Costs:** the least.

A sound choice for a first wig or one you'll wear now and then.

## Monofilament

A fine, see-through mesh at the top of the cap, with each hair tied in by hand. Your scalp, or the cap liner, shows through, so the parting looks like hair growing from skin.

You'll see three versions:

- **Mono part:** mesh along the parting line only.
- **Mono crown:** a small circle at the crown.
- **Mono top:** the whole top. Jon Renau describes this as allowing parting in any direction while looking like natural growth.

**Double monofilament** adds a second soft layer underneath, so the knots don't touch your skin. It's worth knowing about if your scalp is bare or sore.

## Lace front

A strip of very fine lace along the front hairline, again with hairs tied in one by one. Jon Renau's description is that it mimics a natural hairline.

This is what lets you wear your hair off your face. Without it, the front edge of a wig is a solid band, and you'll want a fringe to cover it.

The lace is delicate. Handle the wig by the sides rather than tugging at the front.

## Hand-tied

Here the whole cap is soft, stretchy mesh with every hair tied in by hand. No wefts at all.

- **Feels:** the softest and lightest against the skin.
- **Looks:** the hair moves freely in every direction, like your own.
- **Costs:** the most, because of the hours of work.

If you've no hair underneath, this is the cap most women find kindest.

## How they compare

| Cap | Natural parting | Natural hairline | Coolness | Softness on a bare scalp | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wefted | No | No | Best | Fair | Lowest |
| Mono top, wefted sides | Yes | No | Good | Good | Middle |
| Lace front and mono top | Yes | Yes | Good | Good | Higher |
| Fully hand-tied | Yes | Usually | Good | Best | Highest |

## Which should you choose?

- **Tight budget, or you want a fringe anyway:** wefted.
- **You want a parting that convinces:** mono part or mono top.
- **You want your hair swept back:** lace front.
- **Total hair loss, a tender scalp or all-day wear:** hand-tied, or double monofilament.

A small thing that helps whatever the cap: Macmillan suggests a thin cotton liner if the lining irritates your scalp. It can make the wig shift a little, so you may need to try one or two.

## Reading a product description

Brands list cap features in a row, something like "lace front, monofilament top, hand-tied, velvet ear tabs, adjustable tabs". Once you know the four terms above, the rest is detail: ear tabs help you centre the wig, and adjustable tabs let you tighten it at the nape.
