Yes, you can get a wig on the NHS. A lot of women don't find that out until after they've paid for one, because nobody thought to mention it.

How it works depends on which part of the UK you live in.

## The short answer

| Where you live | What you pay |
|---|---|
| Scotland | Free |
| Wales | Free, within a set budget |
| Northern Ireland | Free, through Health and Social Care services |
| England | A set charge, unless you qualify for a free wig |

## England

### The charges

From April 2026:

| Type of wig | Charge |
|---|---|
| Stock modacrylic (synthetic) wig | £80.15 |
| Partial human hair wig | £212.35 |
| Full bespoke human hair wig | £310.55 |

### Who gets one free

According to the NHS, you don't pay if you:

- are under 16
- are 16 to 18 and in full-time education
- are a hospital inpatient
- are a war pensioner, hold a valid war pension exemption certificate, and the wig is for your accepted disablement
- or your partner get income-related Employment and Support Allowance, Pension Credit Guarantee Credit, or Universal Credit and meet the criteria
- are under 20 and the dependant of someone getting one of those benefits
- hold a valid HC2 certificate from the NHS Low Income Scheme

If you have an HC3 certificate you get partial help. You pay the amount on your certificate or the actual charge, whichever is lower.

Not on benefits but money is tight? You can still apply to the Low Income Scheme using form HC1. It's based on your income and outgoings, not on whether you claim anything.

### How to claim

Tell whoever fits your wig that you're entitled to it free, and show your proof: a benefits letter, an exemption certificate or your HC2.

### Already paid?

You can claim it back. Ask the hospital for a receipt showing you paid an NHS charge, then fill in form HC5(W). Cancer Research UK says to send it within three months of getting the wig.

## Scotland

NHS wigs supplied through a hospital are free.

## Wales

NHS wigs are free. Cancer Research UK explains that this is within a set budget, so if you choose a wig that costs more than the budget, you pay the difference. There are special arrangements if you're registered with a GP in Wales but treated in England, so ask your hospital team.

## Northern Ireland

Wigs are provided free through Health and Social Care services.

## How to get one

NHS wigs come through hospital services, not on an ordinary GP prescription. So the person to ask is:

- your **specialist nurse or consultant**, if you're having cancer treatment
- your **dermatologist**, if you have alopecia or another condition causing hair loss

If you see only your GP, ask to be referred.

You'll then be given a prescription, an appointment with a fitter, or a voucher to use with particular suppliers. Hospitals do this differently.

## What you can expect

**Mostly synthetic.** Macmillan says human hair wigs aren't usually prescribed unless you're allergic to synthetic wigs or have a skin condition they might make worse.

**A limited range.** Macmillan notes the choice of NHS wigs may be restricted.

**A limit on numbers.** You may be able to have more than one, but some hospitals cap how many.

**It varies by area.** Alopecia UK has campaigned on this for years. What's on offer differs from one area to the next, particularly for alopecia.

## Making the most of it

- **Ask what the wig is worth.** Alopecia UK suggests finding out the value of the wig your charge buys. A £200 wig for £80.15 is a good deal. One you could buy for less yourself is not.
- **Ask how many you can have a year**, and when you can next apply.
- **Ask whether you can top up.** Some services let you pay extra for a wig outside the standard range.
- **Think about using both.** An NHS wig for everyday and one you buy yourself, [without VAT if you're eligible](/blogs/guides/vat-relief-on-wigs), is a common mix.

## If you're turned down

Ask why, and ask to see the local policy. Charities can help you make your case: Alopecia UK for alopecia, and Macmillan or your hospital's cancer information centre for cancer treatment.
