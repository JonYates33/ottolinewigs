Washing a wig for the first time is nerve-racking. It feels like the sort of thing you could get badly wrong.

You can't, really, as long as you remember three things: cool water, no rubbing, no heat. The whole job takes about fifteen minutes, plus drying overnight.

## How often?

It depends how much you wear it, and the brands don't quite agree.

| Brand | Advice |
|---|---|
| Ellen Wille | About once or twice a week with average daily wear |
| Jon Renau | Every 6 to 8 wears |

In practice, wash it when it starts to look dull, feel a bit stiff, or smell of anything. More often in summer, or if you use styling products. Less if you wear a liner underneath.

Go by your own brand's leaflet where it differs from this guide.

## What you need

- A clean basin or bowl
- Shampoo made for synthetic wigs. Ellen Wille is firm that ordinary hair products aren't suitable for synthetic hair.
- Wig conditioner or balm
- A towel
- A wig stand
- A wide-tooth comb

## Step by step

**1. Detangle first.** Before the wig goes near water, gently work out any tangles with a wide-tooth comb or your fingers, starting at the ends.

**2. Fill the basin with cool or lukewarm water.** Never hot. Add a small amount of wig shampoo. Ellen Wille suggests a blob about the size of a hazelnut to a litre of water.

**3. Put the wig in and leave it.** Give it a brief shake first, then lower it in and let it soak for about ten minutes. Swish it gently once or twice. Don't scrub.

**4. Rinse.** Use clean cool or lukewarm water until it runs clear. Ellen Wille's instruction here is blunt: do not rub or wring.

**5. Condition.** Ellen Wille recommends a second bath with a little balm for another ten minutes, then a thorough rinse. Jon Renau uses a leave-in conditioning spray, a few pumps on the lengths, kept away from the cap.

**6. Blot.** Lay the wig on a towel and pat it gently. Jon Renau says to wrap it in a towel and pat dry. Never rub.

**7. Dry on a stand.** Put it on a wig stand and leave it to dry in the air, away from radiators and sunshine. It'll take several hours, so overnight is easiest.

**8. Shake and wear.** Once it's fully dry, give it a shake. Macmillan describes the style as heat-sealed into the fibre, and Ellen Wille says the wig returns to its original shape on its own.

## Combing: wet or dry?

The brands differ. Ellen Wille says not to comb or brush a synthetic wig while it's wet or damp. Jon Renau says you can detangle gently with a wide-tooth comb after towelling. If your wig is neither brand, wait until it's dry. It's the cautious choice.

## Five ways to ruin a synthetic wig

1. **Hot water.** It can relax the style for good.
2. **A hairdryer, straighteners or tongs.** Jon Renau's guide is clear: do not use heat to style synthetic fibre. Macmillan warns that even the blast from an open oven can frizz it.
3. **Wringing or rubbing.** It roughs up the fibre and causes frizz.
4. **Ordinary shampoo and conditioner.** They're made for hair that grows, not fibre.
5. **Brushing it hard while wet.** The fibre stretches.

## Heat-friendly wigs

Wash them the same way. The only difference is that once dry, you can style them with tools on the low temperature your brand allows.

## Between washes

- Keep it on a stand, not in a heap.
- Comb the nape gently after each wear. That's where tangles start.
- A light mist of wig conditioning spray on the ends keeps longer styles soft.
