It's real hair, so you'd think you could treat it like your own. Not quite. The hair on a wig has been processed, and it gets none of the natural oil a scalp provides. Jon Renau puts it plainly: it has to be cared for differently from hair that grows from someone's head.

That means gentler washing, special products and a bit more patience. In return you get hair you can style any way you like.

## Before the first wear

A human hair wig isn't always ready to wear straight from the box the way a synthetic one is. Expect to wash and style it first.

Jon Renau also recommends a "check wash" soon after it arrives, within 10 to 14 days depending on which of its guides you read. If the hair tangles or mats badly on that first wash, contact the retailer straight away.

## How often?

Jon Renau recommends every six to eight wears. It also points out a common mistake: people wash less often to make the wig last, but built-up oil and product break down the hair and the cap.

Ellen Wille says simply that it depends on how intensively you wear it.

## What you need

- Shampoo and conditioner made for human hair wigs. Both brands say ordinary salon or supermarket products aren't suitable.
- A wide-tooth comb
- A towel
- A wig stand
- A heat protectant spray, if you'll use tools

## Washing

**1. Detangle while dry.** Work gently from the ends up with a wide-tooth comb.

**2. Wet the hair under cool running water**, letting the water run from the top down so the hair all lies in one direction.

**3. Shampoo.** Smooth it down the lengths with your hands. Don't pile the hair up or scrub it the way you would on your own head.

**4. Rinse** under clean water, again from the top down, until no shampoo is left.

**5. Condition the lengths and ends only.** Keep conditioner away from the base of the cap. Wig Studio 1's Jon Renau instructions explain why: conditioner on the base can weaken the knots that hold the hair in.

**6. Rinse well.**

**7. Blot with a towel.** Wrap and pat. Ellen Wille says to dab gently. Don't rub or wring.

**8. Comb, don't brush.** Jon Renau says never to use a brush on a wet human hair wig. Use a wide-tooth comb, gently.

## Drying and styling

Leave it to dry on a wig stand, or blow-dry it. Unlike synthetic, human hair usually looks best with some styling.

- **Use a heat protectant** every time.
- **Keep the temperature down.** Jon Renau's limit is 350°F, about 175°C. That's lower than many straighteners go, so use tools with a temperature dial.
- **Let it cool** before you move it. Curls set as they cool.

## Colouring and perming

Jon Renau doesn't recommend colouring or chemically altering its wigs. The hair has already been processed, and more can damage it. If you really want a colour change, talk to a hairdresser who works with wigs, and expect that it may affect any guarantee.

## Getting help

Macmillan notes that some human hair wigs need professional cleaning and styling, which can be expensive, and suggests owning two so you have one to wear while the other is away. A salon used to wigs can also do a deep condition when the hair starts to feel dry.

## Habits that make it last

- Store it on a stand, away from sunlight.
- Don't sleep or shower in it.
- Detangle after every wear, starting at the ends.
- Go easy on heat. Every pass of the straighteners shortens its life a little.

With care, Cancer Research UK says a human hair wig typically lasts one to three years. See [how long do wigs last?](/blogs/guides/how-long-do-wigs-last)
