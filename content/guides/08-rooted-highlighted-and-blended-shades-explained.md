Nobody's hair is one colour. Look closely at anyone's and you'll see darker bits underneath, lighter bits at the ends, and a different tone again at the roots.

Wig makers copy that in different ways, and each has a name. Once you know them, colour charts get much easier to read.

## Solid

One colour from root to tip. It's the simplest and usually the cheapest to make.

On very dark hair it looks fine, because natural black and dark brown hair is fairly even. On blondes and mid-browns a solid shade can look flat, which is one of the things that makes a wig look like a wig.

## Blended, or "mix"

Two or more shades woven evenly through the whole wig. From a distance it reads as one colour. Up close it has the depth of natural hair.

Jon Renau marks these with a B. Ellen Wille calls them Mix. If you want your wig to pass unnoticed, a blend is the safest place to start.

## Highlighted

A base colour with lighter strands running through it, like foils from the salon. The effect is brighter and more "done".

Jon Renau's RH shades are about a third highlights. Its Fashion Syrup (FS) colours are bolder still. Lovely if you've always had highlights. A bit much if you never have.

## Rooted, or shaded

The hair is darker at the root and lighter along the length, the way coloured hair looks a few weeks after the salon.

Jon Renau says it found this gives the most appealing look of coloured hair with natural regrowth. You'll see it as S in Jon Renau codes, SS in Raquel Welch and Gabor, and Rooted in Ellen Wille names.

**Why women like it:** the darker root gives depth at the parting and makes a blonde look believable.

**When to think twice:** if you're very fair with pale brows, a strong dark root can look harsh. Look for a soft root, only a shade or two darker than the lengths.

## Tipped

The reverse of rooted. The ends are lighter than the rest, as if the sun has caught them. Jon Renau marks blended and tipped shades BT. It gives a soft, summery look and suits layered cuts.

## Softer effects

Ellen Wille uses a few more words. **Lighted** and **Softed** shades have lighter tones scattered through to lift the colour and add texture. **Toned** and **Shaded** describe gentler shifts between root and length.

## Which looks most natural?

There isn't one answer, but there's a pattern:

- **Dark hair:** a solid or a subtle blend.
- **Brown to dark blonde:** a blend, or a soft root.
- **Blonde:** rooted or blended. A solid pale blonde is the hardest to carry off.
- **Grey:** a mix of several greys, never one flat silver. See [grey and silver wigs](/blogs/guides/grey-and-silver-wigs).

And the simple test: what did your own hair do? If you coloured it and had regrowth, rooted will look like you. If you never coloured it, a blend will.

Macmillan's advice to stay near your own colour, or slightly lighter, still applies whichever effect you pick. For reading the codes themselves, see [how to choose a wig colour](/blogs/guides/how-to-choose-a-wig-colour).
