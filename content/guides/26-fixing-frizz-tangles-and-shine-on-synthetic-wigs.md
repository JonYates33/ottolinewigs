Every synthetic wig frizzes in the end. It's not a fault, and it's not something you did. Simply Wigs says as much: all wigs will eventually frizz, but with the right care you can put it off for a long time.

And when it does happen, there's a good deal you can do before giving up on the wig.

## Why it happens

Wigs.com explains it simply. Synthetic fibre frizzes from friction and tension: when it rubs against your body and clothing, and when you drag a brush through tangles.

That's why it always starts in the same places. The nape, where the wig meets your collar. The ends of longer styles, which brush your shoulders all day. Headcovers adds car headrests and sofa backs to the list.

## Getting tangles out

Do this on a dry wig, on a stand or held firmly in your hand.

1. **Spray the tangled part lightly** with a detangling or conditioning spray made for wigs.
2. **Start at the very ends.** Comb out the last inch or two with a wide-tooth comb.
3. **Work upwards a little at a time.** Never start at the top and pull down. That tightens every knot below it.
4. **Hold the hair above where you're combing**, so you're not tugging at the cap.
5. **Use your fingers on stubborn knots** to tease them apart first.

Wigs.com warns against ordinary hairbrushes on synthetic fibre. They put tension on it, and that causes more frizz.

## Dealing with frizz

**Trim the ends.** If the frizz is only at the tips, the simplest fix is to snip it off. Simply Wigs notes that removing frizz at the tips extends a wig's wearable life. A hairdresser used to wigs will do it more evenly than kitchen scissors.

**Wash and condition.** A proper wash with wig conditioner often softens mild frizz. See [how to wash a synthetic wig](/blogs/guides/how-to-wash-a-synthetic-wig).

**Steam.** Many wearers use a handheld clothes steamer to smooth frizz at the nape and ends, combing gently as the steam passes over. It can work very well. But steam is hot and brands differ on whether they advise it, so check your brand's guidance first, keep the steamer moving, and try a small hidden section before doing more.

**Heat-friendly wigs only:** these can be smoothed with straighteners on the low setting the brand allows. Never try this on ordinary synthetic fibre. Jon Renau's guide is plain that heat must not be used on it.

## What not to do

- **Don't use fabric softener.** You'll see it recommended online. Wig communities warn against it, and no brand we've found advises it.
- **Don't use ordinary hair serums or oils.** They sit on the fibre and attract dust.
- **Don't brush hard.** It's the quickest way to make frizz worse.

## Too shiny?

New synthetic wigs can look glossier than real hair, especially in dark shades and under bright light.

- It usually settles after the first wash or two.
- Many wearers dust on a very small amount of translucent powder or dry shampoo and brush it through. Use less than you think you need.
- When you buy your next one, look for fibres described as matte or low-shine.

## Stopping it coming back

- Comb the nape gently after every wear. Headcovers says this one habit prevents most matting.
- Check the nape and ends regularly, as wigs.com suggests, and deal with frizz while it's small.
- Wear longer styles up or forward over one shoulder with coats and scarves.
- Choose smooth collars over rough wool where you can.
- Don't sleep in it.

## When to let it go

If the nape is matted solid and the ends are rough after washing, trimming and steaming, the wig has done its work. Keep it for the garden or the gym. For what to expect from the next one, see [how long do wigs last?](/blogs/guides/how-long-do-wigs-last)
