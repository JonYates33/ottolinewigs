If your hair is thinning, a full wig can feel like too much. You still have hair. You just want it to look the way it used to.

That's what a topper is for. It's a smaller hairpiece that clips onto the top of your head and blends with your own hair. But it isn't right for everyone, and choosing the wrong one can make thinning worse.

One thing first. If your hair loss is sudden, patchy or comes with a sore scalp, see your GP before buying anything. There may be a cause that can be treated.

## The short version

| | Topper | Full wig |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | The top and parting | Everything |
| Needs | Healthy hair to clip into | Nothing |
| Blending | You match colour and texture to your own hair | No matching needed |
| Feels | Light, your own hair still shows | More coverage, more warmth |
| Best for | Thinning on top with good hair round the sides | Thinning all over, or little hair left |

## A topper will probably suit you if

- Your thinning is mainly at the parting, the crown or the top.
- The hair around the sides and back is still fairly thick and strong.
- You're happy to match a colour to your own hair.
- You want something light that you can take off easily.

Women's hair loss usually shows at the parting first, which is why toppers work so well for it.

## A wig will probably suit you better if

- Your hair is thin all over, including the sides and back.
- The hair where the clips would sit is fine or fragile.
- Your scalp is tender.
- You're about to have treatment that will cause more hair loss.
- You'd rather not fuss with matching and blending.

## Why the clips matter

This is the part to take seriously. A topper is held by small pressure clips. They need healthy hair to grip.

Jon Renau warns that the right base size matters partly so that you aren't pulling on your own hair and causing further loss. Wigs.com puts it more bluntly: clip a topper into hair that's inside or right beside the thinning area and you'll likely speed the loss up.

So you choose a topper that's bigger than the thin patch, so the clips land in strong hair beyond it. If there isn't strong hair beyond it, a topper isn't the kind choice.

## More thinning doesn't always mean a wig

Toppers come in a wide range of sizes. Jon Renau makes the point that being at an advanced stage of hair loss doesn't automatically mean you need a full wig. The larger bases cover most of the top of the head.

What decides it is less the amount of thinning than whether there's sound hair around the edge to hold the clips.

## Can you use both?

Plenty of women do. A topper for most days, and a wig for when their hair is having a bad spell, or for occasions when they want not to think about it.
