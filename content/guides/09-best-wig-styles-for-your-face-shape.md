Face shape advice can sound like a rulebook. It isn't. It's a set of hints hairdressers use to work out why one cut flatters you and another doesn't.

Use it to narrow hundreds of styles down to a dozen. Then choose the one you like.

## Find your shape in a minute

Pull your hair back and look straight into a mirror. Notice three things: where your face is widest, the shape of your jaw, and whether your face is longer than it is wide.

| Shape | What you'll see |
|---|---|
| Oval | Slightly longer than wide, forehead a little wider than the chin, soft jaw |
| Round | About as wide as it is long, full cheeks, soft jaw |
| Square | Forehead, cheeks and jaw similar in width, a strong jawline |
| Heart | Wider forehead, narrowing to a small chin |
| Long | Clearly longer than wide, forehead and jaw similar in width |

Plenty of women are between two. That's fine. Read both.

## What tends to suit each

**Oval.** Most things. Short crops, bobs, long layers. You can choose on taste alone.

**Round.** Styles that add a little height at the crown and keep the sides close. Layers that fall below the chin lengthen the face. A side parting helps. A blunt chin-length bob with a heavy fringe tends to widen it.

**Square.** Softness around the jaw. Waves, wispy layers and side-swept fringes. A length that ends above or below the jaw, not right on it.

**Heart.** Width lower down. Chin-length bobs and layers that flick out around the jaw balance a wider forehead. A soft or side-swept fringe helps too.

**Long.** Width at the sides and a fringe to shorten the face. Bobs, waves and curls work well. Very long, straight styles with no fringe make a long face look longer.

## Things that matter as much as shape

**Your hairline.** A fringe hides the front edge of a wig, which makes almost any cap look natural. If you want your hair off your face, you'll want a lace front. See [cap constructions explained](/blogs/guides/wig-cap-constructions-explained).

**Glasses.** Heavy fringes and frames compete. A side-swept fringe sits better.

**Volume.** Macmillan suggests a wig with the same volume as your own hair or slightly less. Too much hair overwhelms a face faster than the wrong cut does.

**What you're used to.** Cancer Research UK suggests taking photos of your hair as you normally wear it. A style close to your own is the one you'll forget you're wearing.

## A wig can be cut

This is the part people don't realise. If a style is nearly right, a hairdresser who works with wigs can take length off, thin it, or cut in a fringe. Macmillan mentions that My New Hair lists salons trained to do it.

One caution: cut hair doesn't grow back, and most retailers won't take a cut wig back. Wear it for a few days first so you're sure about the colour and fit.

## If you're trying something new

Losing your hair can be a chance to try the cut you never dared to. If you fancy that, go for it. But for a first wig, many women find something close to their usual look easier to get used to, and save the experiment for the second.
