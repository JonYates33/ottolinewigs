Colour is the thing women worry about most, and with reason. You're choosing from a thumbnail on a phone, and "Sandy Blonde Rooted" tells you very little.

The good news is that there's a method, and the codes aren't as cryptic as they look.

## Start with what you know

For a first wig, stay close to your own colour. Macmillan suggests matching it or going a shade lighter. Lighter is kinder than darker, especially if you've always been fair.

If you're having treatment, Cancer Research UK points out that your complexion may change a little, so ask a fitter what will still suit you in a few months.

## See it in real life if you can

Screens lie. The same shade looks different on every phone, and different again by a window.

- **Borrow a colour ring or order swatches.** Several UK retailers offer this. It's the single best thing you can do before a first order.
- **Look at it in daylight**, next to your face, not under a kitchen light.
- **Search the shade name plus "review".** Photos and videos from real wearers show a colour far better than the brand's own picture.

## The same name isn't always the same colour

This catches people out. A brand may use one colour name across dozens of styles, but the blend is adjusted for each cut. So a shade you loved in a bob can look lighter or more streaked in a long layered style. Always look at the colour on the actual wig you're buying.

## How to read a colour code

Every brand has its own system. Here are the ones you'll meet most.

### Jon Renau

Numbers tell you the colour family. Letters tell you how the colours are put together.

| Numbers | Family |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Blacks |
| 4 to 12 | Browns |
| 14 to 26 | Blondes |
| 27 to 33, and 130 to 134 | Reds |
| 34 to 60, and 92 | Greys |
| 101 to 104, and 613 | Platinums |

| Letter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| B | Colours blended evenly |
| BT | Blended, with lighter tips |
| F | Blended, with the darker colour solid at the nape |
| RH | Blended with about a third highlights |
| S | Shaded, meaning a darker root |
| FS | "Fashion Syrup", a bold salon-style highlight |

So **14/26S10** is a mix of blondes 14 and 26, shaded with a darker root in brown 10.

### Ellen Wille

Ellen Wille uses a name followed by a word that describes the effect: **Mix** (several tones blended), **Rooted** (darker at the root), **Lighted**, **Tipped**, **Toned**, **Shaded** or **Softed**. You'll often see numbers too, such as 12.26.27, which list the shades in the blend.

### Raquel Welch and Gabor

Both use **SS** for a shaded, darker root. RL12/22SS, for instance, is a rooted version of the 12/22 blend.

## If you're stuck between two

- Go for the lighter one.
- Choose the one with more than a single flat tone. Blends and rooted shades look more like real hair.
- Buy from a retailer whose returns policy lets you send back an unworn wig, and check the colour in daylight before you take off the tags.
