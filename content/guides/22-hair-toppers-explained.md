A topper is a small hairpiece that clips onto the top of your head. It covers thinning at the parting or crown and blends into your own hair, so what people see is simply fuller hair.

If you're not sure whether you need one or a full wig, read [wig or topper?](/blogs/guides/thinning-hair-wig-or-topper) first.

## Base size is everything

The base is the part that sits on your head. Get the size right and the rest is easy. Get it wrong and the topper either won't cover enough or will pull on fragile hair.

Headcovers groups them like this:

| Coverage | Typical base | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Part and hairline | up to about 4 by 5 inches | Early thinning, a widening parting |
| Crown and top | about 5 by 6 to 6.5 by 7 inches | Moderate thinning, scalp showing through |
| Full top | larger still | Advanced thinning across the whole top |

## How to measure

1. In good light, find the area where your hair is thin. A second mirror or a photo helps.
2. Measure it front to back, then side to side.
3. Add a margin all the way round.

That margin is the important bit. The clips have to sit in healthy hair beyond the thin patch. Wigs.com suggests at least a quarter of an inch over. Wig Studio 1 recommends an inch all round. If your hair near the edge is fine, go for the bigger margin.

If you fall between two sizes, wigs.com's advice is to choose the slightly larger base.

## Why it matters so much

Jon Renau says the right base size makes the piece look more natural and also makes sure you aren't pulling on your own hair and causing more loss. A topper that's too small puts the clips right where your hair is weakest.

And if there's very little hair for a clip to hold, wigs.com says not to use clips there at all. That's the point at which a wig is kinder.

## Other choices

**The base material.** A monofilament base shows what looks like scalp at the parting. Lace at the front gives a soft hairline. Hand-tied bases are the softest.

**The fibre.** Synthetic keeps its style and costs less. Human hair can be styled with your own and blends more easily if your hair has a wave. See [synthetic vs human hair](/blogs/guides/synthetic-vs-human-hair-vs-heat-friendly-wigs).

**The length.** Match your own hair, or go a little longer and have it trimmed to blend.

## Matching the colour

A topper sits next to your real hair, so colour matters more than with a wig.

- Match your **roots and mid-lengths**, not your ends.
- If you colour your hair, match what it looks like most of the time, not the day after the salon.
- A rooted shade often blends best, because the darker root melts into your own.
- Use a colour ring or swatches if you possibly can. See [how to choose a wig colour](/blogs/guides/how-to-choose-a-wig-colour).

## How to clip one in

1. Part your own hair where you want the topper's parting to sit.
2. Open all the clips.
3. Place the front edge a little way behind your hairline, so some of your own hair falls in front.
4. Fasten the front clip, then the sides, then the back.
5. Blend with your fingers or a wide-tooth comb.

Wigs.com has one firm rule here. Don't pull the topper tight as you fasten each clip. Press down on the middle of the piece instead, so there's no tension on your hair.

To take it off, undo the clips from back to front and lift it away gently.

## Looking after your own hair

- Move the position slightly from day to day so the clips don't always grip the same hair.
- Take it off at night.
- If you notice more thinning where the clips sit, stop and move up a base size, or switch to a wig.

Caring for the topper itself is the same as for a wig of the same fibre. See [how to wash a synthetic wig](/blogs/guides/how-to-wash-a-synthetic-wig).
