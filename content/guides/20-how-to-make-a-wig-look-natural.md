Here's something worth knowing before anything else. Nobody is looking at your hair as closely as you are. People notice a face, a smile, a general impression of nice hair. They don't inspect partings.

That said, a few small things separate a wig that looks like hair from one that looks like a wig. Nearly all of them can be fixed, and most cost nothing.

## 1. It's sitting too low

The commonest giveaway by far. A wig pulled down towards the eyebrows looks heavy and hat-like. It should sit at your natural hairline, roughly four fingers above your brows. See [how to put on a wig](/blogs/guides/how-to-put-on-a-wig-and-keep-it-secure).

## 2. There's too much hair

Wigs come out of the box fuller than most heads of hair. Macmillan's advice is to choose the same volume as your own hair or slightly less.

If yours is too full, a hairdresser who works with wigs can thin it. Macmillan notes that wigs can be cut and styled by trained hairdressers, and that My New Hair lists salons that do it.

## 3. It's too perfect

Real hair is never quite tidy. A wig with every strand in place looks unreal.

Shake it out before you put it on. Use your fingers instead of a comb. Tuck one side behind your ear. Let a few bits fall where they like.

## 4. The hairline is a hard edge

Unless your wig has a lace front, the front edge is a solid line. There are three ways round it.

- Wear a fringe, even a wispy one, which hides the edge completely.
- Choose a lace-front wig next time. See [cap constructions](/blogs/guides/wig-cap-constructions-explained).
- If you have some hair of your own at the front, pull a little forward over the edge of the wig.

## 5. The colour is flat

One solid colour is rare in nature. Blended and rooted shades have the depth real hair has. See [rooted, highlighted and blended shades](/blogs/guides/rooted-highlighted-and-blended-shades-explained).

And remember that price doesn't decide this. Cancer Research UK says plainly that paying more doesn't mean a more natural look.

## 6. It's shiny

New synthetic wigs can catch the light more than hair does. It tends to settle after a wash or two. Many wearers also dust a very little translucent powder or dry shampoo over the top and brush it through. Go lightly. It should take the shine off, not leave a film.

## 7. The parting looks wrong

A basic wefted cap has a fixed parting with a little lift to hide the wefts. A monofilament top shows something that looks like scalp, and lets you move the parting.

If your parting line looks too neat and straight, break it up. A slightly zig-zag parting looks more like the real thing.

## 8. It doesn't suit you

Sometimes the wig is fine and it's simply not your style. Macmillan suggests taking a friend or relative when you choose, for an honest opinion. It's good advice. We're poor judges of ourselves in a mirror on a stressful day.

## The single best thing you can do

Have it cut. A trim to suit your face, a bit of thinning, a fringe shaped properly. It's the difference between a wig and your wig.

Wear it for a few days first, to be sure of the colour and fit, because a cut wig usually can't be returned.

## Give it time

The last thing is you. A wig looks most natural on someone who has stopped thinking about it. That comes with wearing it: round the house, then to the shops, then out with friends. By the second week most women find they've forgotten it's there.
