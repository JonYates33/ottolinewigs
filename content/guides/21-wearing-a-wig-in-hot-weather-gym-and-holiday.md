A wig is a hat you can't take off in public, or that's how it can feel in July. The worry is real, but so are the ways round it. Women wear wigs in hot countries, in the gym and on the beach all the time.

## Staying cool

**Pick a lighter wig for summer.** Simply Wigs points out that synthetic fibre is lighter than human hair, so it keeps your head cooler. Human hair can also go limp or frizz in humidity, just as your own would.

**Choose an airy cap.** Wefted caps have gaps between the rows of hair that let air through. Alopecia UK's guide describes them as light with extra air circulation.

**Go shorter, or wear it up.** Hair off your neck makes more difference than you'd expect.

**Wear something underneath.** A cotton or bamboo liner soaks up sweat. A wig grip band can help too. Simply Wigs says many of its customers find one keeps them cooler.

**Wash it more often.** Sweat builds up in the cap. In hot weather you'll want to wash your wig more than usual.

## When it's simply too hot

You're allowed a day off. Turbans and headscarves in breathable fabrics like bamboo are far cooler than any wig, and Simply Wigs suggests them as a summer alternative.

One Alopecia UK writer describes finding it too hot for her wig on holiday in Mexico and worrying about going without. Her family's answer was that everybody was too busy enjoying their own holiday to care. It's worth remembering.

## Exercise

For a gentle walk or a yoga class, a short lightweight wig with a grip band is usually fine. For anything that gets you properly hot, most women switch to a headband, cap or sports turban. It's more comfortable, and it saves your wig from sweat.

If you'd rather wear hair, keep an older wig for the gym.

## Swimming

Chlorine, salt water and sun are hard on wig fibre.

- **Easiest:** swap the wig for a swim cap or a swimming turban.
- **If you want to swim in a wig:** use an older one you don't mind about. Joseph's Wigs' advice is to keep your newest away from the pool.
- **Afterwards:** rinse it in clean water as soon as you can, then wash it properly.
- **Don't assume your usual tape will hold in water.** Simply Wigs advises checking the manufacturer's guidance first.

## Packing

**How many?** Joseph's Wigs suggests three for a holiday: one for the day, one for evenings, and an older one for the pool. Two is plenty for most trips.

**How to pack.** Put the wig in hand luggage if you can, so it doesn't go missing with a suitcase. A wig box keeps the shape best. Without one, turn the wig gently inside out, tuck the ends in and put it in a bag on top of your clothes.

**Take:** a folding stand, a wide-tooth comb, travel-size wig shampoo and a spare liner.

## Where to keep it when you're away

Simply Wigs' travel guide warns against three things: leaving your wig in a hot car, on a sunny windowsill, or packed away while it's still damp. Heat can spoil synthetic fibre, and a damp wig in a closed bag goes musty.

## At the airport

Wigs go through security like anything else you're wearing, and you wouldn't normally be asked to take one off. If you're ever asked for a closer check and would prefer privacy, you can ask for it to be done in a private room.
