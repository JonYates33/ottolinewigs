Buy a wig and you'll be offered a dozen extras. Some are close to essential. Some you'll never use. Which is which depends mostly on whether you have hair underneath.

## Things worn under the wig

### Wig cap

A thin stretchy cap, usually nylon or mesh, that holds your own hair flat.

**You need one if** you have hair, especially long hair. **You don't if** you have none. On a bare scalp a nylon cap can be slippery and warm.

### Cotton or bamboo liner

A soft cap that sits between your scalp and the wig. Macmillan recommends a thin cotton liner if the wig's lining irritates you. It should be washed every day, and it can make the wig sit or move a little differently.

**You need one if** your scalp is tender or you find the cap scratchy. Have two or three so there's always a clean one.

### Wig grip band

A soft band, often velvet, that fastens round your hairline under the wig and stops it sliding.

**You need one if** your wig moves and you'd rather not use tape. Simply Wigs says many of its customers find a grip band also helps them stay cooler, by letting sweat evaporate.

**You may not if** you find any pressure round the head uncomfortable. Some women get headaches from them.

## Things that stick

### Wig tape

Double-sided tape that holds the wig to your skin. Macmillan suggests hypoallergenic tape made for wigs.

**You need it if** you have no hair and want to feel completely secure, on a windy day for instance. Try it on a small patch of skin first. If you're having treatment, ask your doctor or nurse before using it.

### Adhesive pads

Cancer Research UK mentions these for when all your hair has gone. They do the same job as tape in a different form.

### Glue

Liquid adhesive, used mostly with lace-front wigs worn for days at a time. Most women wearing a wig they take off each night don't need it, and it's the most likely of the three to irritate skin.

## Things for looking after the wig

These aren't optional, and they don't cost much.

- **A stand.** Elegant Wigs' care instructions say a wig should always dry on a stand and be stored on one to keep its shape. A folding one is fine.
- **A wide-tooth comb.** Ordinary brushes pull at synthetic fibre.
- **Wig shampoo and conditioner** made for your fibre. See [how to wash a synthetic wig](/blogs/guides/how-to-wash-a-synthetic-wig).
- **A conditioning or detangling spray** if your wig is shoulder length or longer.

## What you can probably skip

- **A polystyrene head** for everyday storage. A simple stand lets air through and takes up less room. A head is only useful if you style your wig on it.
- **Special wig hairspray, mousse and shine sprays** until you know you want them.
- **A full "starter kit".** They're often padded out with things you won't use.

## A sensible first shop

**If you have hair:** a wig cap, a stand, a wide-tooth comb, shampoo and conditioner.

**If you have little or no hair:** two cotton liners or a grip band, a stand, a wide-tooth comb, shampoo and conditioner. Add tape later if you find you want it.

That's it. Wear the wig for a fortnight, and you'll know what else, if anything, would help.
