## How to choose

- **Expect a wefted cap.** It is light and airy but has no scalp-like parting. Styles with a fringe or lift at the crown hide that best. See [cap constructions explained](/blogs/guides/wig-cap-constructions-explained).
- **Check the weight.** Under 70g is light. Some budget wigs are much heavier.
- **Check the size.** Most wigs at this price come in average only. Two here are smaller.
- **Stick to known brands.** A branded wig under £100 is a safer buy than an unbranded one at £30. See [what wigs cost in the UK](/blogs/guides/how-much-do-wigs-cost-in-the-uk).
- **Remember VAT relief.** If you qualify, a £120 wig costs £100.

## Who should look elsewhere

- **You want a hairline you can brush back.** You need a lace front, which starts at around £125. See [best wigs for beginners](/blogs/guides/best-wigs-for-beginners).
- **Your scalp is sore or bare.** A softer cap is worth the extra. See [wigs for sensitive scalps](/blogs/guides/wigs-for-sensitive-scalps).
- **You may qualify for an NHS wig.** In England the charge for a stock synthetic wig is lower than any wig here.

## Questions people ask

**Are cheap wigs any good?**

Branded ones are. The fibre is the same quality as in dearer wigs from the same maker. The saving comes from the simpler cap.

**Why not buy a £25 wig from a marketplace?**

You might be lucky, but sizes, colours and fibre quality are a gamble, and returns can be hard.

**How long will a budget wig last?**

About as long as a dearer synthetic one worn the same way. See [how long do wigs last?](/blogs/guides/how-long-do-wigs-last)

**Is it worth buying two?**

Yes, if you wear a wig daily. Two worn in turn last longer, and you have a spare on wash day.
