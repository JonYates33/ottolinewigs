A scalp that's lost its hair is skin that has never been touched before. It feels everything: a seam, a knot, a bit of warmth. During treatment it can be sore before you put anything on it at all.

So if a wig itches or rubs, you're not being fussy. Usually something specific is causing it, and usually it can be fixed.

If your scalp is broken, blistered or has a rash, speak to your nurse, GP or dermatologist before wearing a wig over it.

## What tends to cause the trouble

- **Rough bits inside the cap.** Wefts, seams and the tiny knots where hair is tied in.
- **Heat and sweat.** A warm, damp scalp itches.
- **A cap that's too tight.** Pressure along the hairline and behind the ears.
- **Tape or glue.** Skin can react, especially during treatment.
- **A wig that needs washing.** Sweat, oil and product build up inside the cap.

## Choose a kinder cap

The inside of the wig matters more than anything you add to it.

- **Hand-tied caps** are soft, stretchy mesh with no wefts. Most women find them the gentlest on bare skin.
- **Double monofilament tops** add a smooth layer between the knots and your scalp.
- **Wefted caps** are the airiest. Alopecia UK's guide describes them as light, with good air circulation, and comfortable for a sensitive scalp. If heat is your problem more than rubbing, they can be the better choice.

Our guide to [cap constructions](/blogs/guides/wig-cap-constructions-explained) explains the differences.

## Add a liner

Macmillan suggests a thin cotton liner or skullcap under the wig if the lining irritates your scalp. It puts a soft layer between you and the cap and soaks up sweat.

Two things to know. Wash it every day. And a liner can make the wig sit a little differently or move more, so you may need to try a couple before you find one that works with your wig.

## Small habits that help

These come from Macmillan:

- **Freshen your scalp.** Dab it with a damp cloth, or use an alcohol-free, fragrance-free spray.
- **Give it a rest.** Take the wig off in the evening, at night and in the shower.
- **Be careful with tape.** Use hypoallergenic tape made for wigs, and check with your doctor or nurse before using any adhesive if you're having treatment.

And two more that make a difference:

- **Loosen the adjusters.** Many women wear a wig tighter than they need to. If it passes the bend-and-shake test, it's tight enough.
- **Keep the wig clean.** Ellen Wille's care advice is that how often you wash depends on how much you wear it. If your scalp is reacting, try washing a little more often.

## If you react to synthetic fibre

A few women find synthetic wigs themselves irritate their skin. If that's you, tell your hospital team. Macmillan says the NHS doesn't usually prescribe human hair wigs, but can if you're allergic to synthetic ones or have a skin condition that a synthetic wig might make worse.

## When a wig isn't comfortable at all

Some days nothing feels right, and that's normal. Soft hats, cotton or bamboo turbans and scarves are there for those days. Cancer Research UK has advice on them alongside its wig information.

You don't have to wear a wig every day for it to be worth having.
