The fibre decides more about daily life with a wig than anything else. Not how it looks on day one, since good wigs of every kind look lovely on day one, but what you'll be doing with it every morning.

## At a glance

| | Synthetic | Heat-friendly synthetic | Human hair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning effort | Almost none | A little | Style it like your own |
| Can you use heat? | No | Yes, on low | Yes |
| How long it lasts | Months | Months, often fewer | A year or more |
| Typical cost | Lowest | Low to middle | Highest |
| On the NHS? | Yes | Varies | Rarely |

## Synthetic

This is what most women start with, and plenty never switch.

The style is set into the fibre. Macmillan describes it as heat-sealed: you hand-wash the wig in cool water, leave it to dry overnight and it springs back into shape. Rain doesn't flatten it. Cancer Research UK notes that synthetic wigs need no styling at all.

The catch is heat. An ordinary synthetic wig can't take a hairdryer, straighteners or tongs, and Macmillan warns that even the blast from an open oven door can frizz or melt the fibre. You learn to step back when you open the oven.

How long does one last? The charities give slightly different figures: Macmillan says usually 4 to 8 months, Cancer Research UK says 6 to 9. Either way, if you wear it every day, expect to replace it within the year.

**Good for:** a first wig, everyday wear, anyone who wants to get up and go.

## Heat-friendly synthetic

This is synthetic fibre made to cope with styling tools on a low setting, so you can smooth it or add a wave. The style then stays until you change it.

Two things to know. You have to keep within the temperature the brand gives, which is much lower than you'd use on your own hair. And the fibre tends to tangle and wear faster than ordinary synthetic, especially at the nape on longer styles, so it needs more looking after.

**Good for:** women who like to change their look and don't mind a bit of upkeep.

## Human hair

It moves like hair because it is hair. You can blow-dry it, curl it, even have it coloured by someone who knows wigs. Cancer Research UK says a human hair wig typically lasts one to three years.

But be honest with yourself about the work. It doesn't hold a style through washing, so you're styling it each time. Macmillan points out that some need professional cleaning and styling, which adds up, and suggests having two so you've one to wear while the other is away.

Then there's the price: a few hundred to a few thousand pounds, according to Macmillan. A wig made to order can take two to three months and several fittings.

**Good for:** long-term wearers who enjoy styling and want the most natural movement.

## Blends

Some wigs mix human and synthetic hair. They can be a good middle ground, but Macmillan notes the synthetic strands can stretch and frizz under a hairdryer or straighteners, so treat a blend as gently as you would a synthetic.

## So which one?

- **Losing your hair for a few months during treatment?** Synthetic. It's affordable, easy when you're tired, and available on the NHS.
- **Wearing a wig every day for the long run?** Many women keep an easy synthetic for ordinary days and a human hair wig for when it matters.
- **Sensitive skin?** Tell your hospital team. Macmillan says the NHS can prescribe human hair if you're allergic to synthetic wigs or have a skin condition they'd make worse.

Whatever you choose, use shampoo made for that fibre. Ellen Wille, like most brands, says ordinary hair products aren't suitable for synthetic hair.
