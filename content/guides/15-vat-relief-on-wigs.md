If you wear a wig because of a medical condition, you may be paying more than you need to. VAT adds 20% to the price, and many women with medical hair loss don't have to pay it.

Nobody takes it off for you, though. You have to claim it, and you have to claim it when you buy.

## How much it saves

VAT is 20% on top of the price before tax. So the saving is one sixth of the price you see on the shelf.

| Price including VAT | Price without VAT | You save |
|---|---|---|
| £120 | £100 | £20 |
| £240 | £200 | £40 |
| £600 | £500 | £100 |

Some retailers describe it as "20% off". It's nearer 17% off the ticket price.

## Who qualifies

The relief is for people HMRC calls "chronically sick or disabled". In HMRC's guidance, that means someone with:

- a physical or mental impairment that has a long-term and substantial effect on everyday activities,
- a condition the medical profession treats as a chronic sickness, or
- a terminal illness.

A few things that surprise people:

- **You don't need to be registered disabled**, and it has nothing to do with benefits.
- **It isn't means-tested.**
- **You don't need HMRC's permission.** You make the declaration yourself. HMRC says its advisers can't tell you whether you count as chronically sick or disabled.

### Who doesn't

HMRC says the term doesn't cover someone who is only temporarily disabled or incapacitated, such as with a broken limb, or someone who is elderly but not chronically sick or disabled.

So a wig bought for fashion doesn't qualify. Nor does thinning that comes simply with age, with no underlying condition.

### Hair loss from cancer treatment

Macmillan and Cancer Research UK both say you shouldn't have to pay VAT on a wig bought because of hair loss from cancer treatment.

### Alopecia and other conditions

Wig retailers commonly accept alopecia and other long-term medical causes of hair loss. In the end, though, it's your declaration. If you're unsure whether your condition counts, read HMRC's guidance or ring its helpline before you sign.

## What it covers

Wigs are among the goods HMRC accepts for this relief when they're supplied to an eligible person for their own use.

Retailers generally apply it to wigs and hairpieces, and not to shampoos, brushes, stands, scarves or hats.

Be careful with anything that isn't plainly a wig. HMRC has challenged the relief on some salon-fitted hair replacement systems, and the question has gone to tribunal. If you're buying one of those, ask the provider exactly how they treat the VAT.

## How to claim

1. **Check the retailer offers it.** Macmillan warns that not every supplier does. Look for a VAT relief or VAT exemption page before you shop.
2. **Fill in the declaration.** You give your name and address, describe your condition, and confirm the wig is for your own use. Online it's usually a short form or a tick box at the checkout.
3. **Pay the price without VAT.** Some retailers take it off at the checkout. Others charge the full amount and refund the VAT once they've processed your form.

The retailer keeps your declaration with its records. It doesn't go to HMRC, and you don't need to send anything yourself.

## Do it when you buy

This is the part that catches people. Cancer Research UK is blunt about it: you must fill in the form at the time you buy, because you can't claim the VAT back later.

If you've already bought a wig and paid the VAT, it's worth asking the retailer straight away whether they can still sort it out. But don't count on it.

## A few rules

- The wig has to be for **you**. A parent, spouse or guardian can buy on behalf of someone who qualifies.
- There's **no limit** on how many wigs you can buy this way.
- Making a **false declaration** is a serious matter. Only sign if it's true.

## NHS wig or VAT relief?

They're different things, and you can use both. An NHS wig comes through your hospital at a set charge or free. VAT relief applies to wigs you buy privately. See [NHS wigs](/blogs/guides/nhs-wigs-who-qualifies-and-how-to-get-one).

## If you need to check

HMRC has a helpline for VAT reliefs for disabled people: 0300 123 1073. They can explain the rules, though they can't decide for you whether you're eligible.
