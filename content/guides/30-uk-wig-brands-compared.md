Once you start looking at wigs, the same handful of names comes up everywhere. They're sold by most UK retailers, online and in shops, and between them they cover almost every style, cap and budget.

Here's who they are. One caution first: most of what's written about wig brands comes from the shops that sell them. Treat praise with a pinch of salt, ours included, and judge by the wig on your head.

## At a glance

| Brand | From | Known for |
|---|---|---|
| Ellen Wille | Germany | Natural-looking, comfortable wigs with soft European colouring. Towards the premium end. |
| Jon Renau | USA | A wide range from budget to top end, a huge colour chart, and toppers. |
| Raquel Welch | USA | Glamorous styles and heat-friendly fibre. |
| Gabor | USA | Classic, easy-wear styles at moderate prices. |

## Ellen Wille

A German company founded in 1967 by the hair designer whose name it carries. Wigs.com describes it as having clients in more than 50 countries. Ellen Wille herself died in March 2024, aged 81, and the company continues.

Simply Wigs says the brand is especially popular with wearers who put comfort and a natural appearance first, and that its more sophisticated construction puts it towards the premium end.

**Collections you'll see:** Hairpower, Hair Society, Perucci and Changes.

**Colours:** named shades with words like Mix and Rooted. See [how to choose a wig colour](/blogs/guides/how-to-choose-a-wig-colour).

**Consider it if:** you want something understated that passes as your own hair, and your budget stretches a little.

## Jon Renau

Founded in 1984 by John and Stella Reynolds. The business began in South Africa and is now based in California, and it's still family-owned.

Simply Wigs describes the range as running from the more affordable Classic collection up to SmartLace at the top. The brand is known for its colour system, where the numbers and letters tell you exactly what's in a shade, and for its toppers.

**Consider it if:** you want lots of choice, you like to understand exactly what colour you're buying, or you're looking for a topper. See [hair toppers explained](/blogs/guides/hair-toppers-explained).

## Raquel Welch

The actress launched her wig collection in the late 1990s, and it's now part of the HairUWear group. The styles lean glamorous, and many use the brand's heat-friendly fibre, which can be styled with tools on a low setting.

Shaded, rooted colours are marked SS.

**Consider it if:** you like a polished look and want the option to restyle.

## Gabor

Gabor goes back to 1968, when it began as Eva Gabor's wig company. Name Brand Wigs notes that the business became HairUWear, which now owns several brands including Raquel Welch.

The styles are classic and wearable, and the pricing is generally moderate.

**Consider it if:** you want a comfortable everyday wig without paying top prices.

## Other names worth knowing

Simply Wigs' list of the brands it most often recommends also includes **René of Paris**, **Noriko**, **Amore** and **Gisela Mayer**. All are widely stocked in the UK. Simply Wigs picks out Gisela Mayer for its versatility, with enough variety for first-time and experienced wearers alike.

## How to choose between them

Brand matters less than you'd think. Within any one of these, the gap between the basic and the best wig is bigger than the gap between brands.

So choose in this order:

1. **Cap construction**, for comfort and realism. See [cap constructions explained](/blogs/guides/wig-cap-constructions-explained).
2. **Fibre**, for how you'll live with it.
3. **Style and colour** you like.
4. **Size.** Not every brand makes every style in petite or large.
5. **Then brand**, as a tie-breaker.

If you find a brand whose caps fit you well, stick with it. Sizing and cap shape vary a little from one to another, and a good fit is worth more than a famous name.

## Buying safely

Buy from a retailer that states it's an approved stockist, and be wary of prices far below everyone else's. A genuine wig comes with the brand's label inside the cap and its care leaflet.
