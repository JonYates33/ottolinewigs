A wig that fits stays put and you forget it's there. One that doesn't will ride up at the back or leave a mark across your forehead by lunchtime. Measuring takes about two minutes.

## What you need

A soft fabric tape measure. If you don't have one, use a piece of string and hold it against a ruler afterwards.

Flatten your hair as much as you can first. If it's long, pin it flat or tuck it under a wig cap, the way it'll sit under the wig.

## The three measurements

**1. Around your head.** This is the one that decides your size. Start at your front hairline, take the tape behind one ear, round the nape of your neck, behind the other ear and back to the front. Keep it snug, not tight.

**2. Front to back.** From your front hairline, straight over the top of your head, down to the nape where your hairline ends.

**3. Ear to ear.** From the top of one ear, over the crown, to the top of the other.

Do each one twice. If the numbers disagree, do it a third time.

## Size chart

| Size | Around the head |
|---|---|
| Petite | about 53 to 54 cm (21 to 21.5 in) |
| Average | about 55 to 57 cm (21.5 to 22.5 in) |
| Large | about 58 to 60 cm (23 to 23.5 in) |

These are typical figures. Every brand cuts slightly differently, so always check the brand's own chart before you buy.

Most wigs are made in average only, because that's what most women are. Petite and large styles exist but there are fewer to choose from, so it's worth knowing your size before you fall for a style.

## If you're between sizes

Nearly every wig has adjusters at the nape, usually small Velcro tabs or hooks, that take the cap in or let it out by a centimetre or two. So:

- **Just under average?** An average cap tightened up will usually do.
- **Just over average?** Try an average let fully out before going to large. A large cap on a not-quite-large head will slip.

## If you're about to lose your hair

This catches a lot of women out. If you measure with a full head of hair, the wig will be loose once that hair has gone.

Both Macmillan and Cancer Research UK say the same thing: you may need a smaller size later, so choose a wig that adjusts. Measure with your hair as flat as you can get it, and pick a cap with room to tighten.

## Signs the fit is wrong

- It creeps back and shows your hairline: too small, or sitting too far back.
- The ear tabs don't sit level in front of your ears: wrong size, or on crooked.
- It lifts or shifts when you bend forward: too big. Tighten the adjusters first.
- You get a headache after an hour or two: too tight.

Macmillan's test is a good one. Bend over and give your head a gentle shake. If the wig stays where it is, you're fine.
