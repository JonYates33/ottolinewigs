With your own hair, length is about how you want to look. With a wig, it's also about how much work you're signing up for.

That isn't a reason to go short if you love long hair. It's a reason to choose with your eyes open.

## What length changes

**Tangling.** This is the big one for synthetic wigs. The fibre frizzes from friction, and longer hair rubs against more: collars, scarves, coat hoods, the back of the sofa, the car headrest. The nape and the ends go first.

**How long it lasts.** A short wig simply has less hair in harm's way. Simply Wigs notes that shorter styles are generally easier to care for, though the nape can still frizz.

**Weight and warmth.** More hair is heavier and warmer. You notice it in summer and at the end of a long day.

**Daily effort.** A short synthetic style needs a shake and your fingers. A long one needs detangling after every wear.

## Short (crops and pixies)

**For:** the lightest, coolest and lowest effort. Often the most convincing too, because there's less to move unnaturally.

**Against:** a big change if you've always had long hair. Less to hide behind.

**Good if:** you want easy, you run warm, or you've worn short hair before.

## Mid (bobs and shoulder length)

**For:** the best of both. Enough hair to frame your face, not enough to tangle badly. Chin-length bobs that clear the collar last particularly well.

**Against:** styles that sit right on the shoulder rub with every turn of the head.

**Good if:** it's your first wig and you're not sure. This is where most women land.

## Long (past the shoulders)

**For:** it's how you feel like yourself, and that matters a great deal.

**Against:** the most upkeep and, in synthetic, the shortest life. Heat-friendly fibre tangles faster still.

**Good if:** you're willing to detangle after each wear, or you're choosing human hair, which copes with length better.

## Making long hair work

If you want length, a few habits make a real difference:

- Detangle the nape after every wear with a wide-tooth comb. Headcovers' care guide says a gentle pass each time stops it building into something harder to fix.
- Use a conditioning spray made for wigs on the ends.
- Wear it up or over one shoulder when you're in a coat or scarf.
- Keep a shorter wig for everyday and save the long one for when it counts.

## Match what you have, or nearly

Macmillan's advice for a first wig is to stay close to your own style. If your hair has always been long, going a few inches shorter than you're used to gives you most of the look with far less tangling. Collarbone length instead of mid-back, say.

## A simple way to decide

Ask yourself how you'll feel on an ordinary tired Tuesday. If five minutes of detangling sounds fine, go as long as you like. If it sounds like one more thing, choose the length that asks least of you. You can always have a longer one as well.
