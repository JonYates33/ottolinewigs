Grey is harder to get right than blonde or brown, for a simple reason. Nobody's grey is one colour. It's white hairs mixed with whatever you had before, in a proportion that's yours alone.

A wig in a single flat silver looks like a costume. A wig that copies your mix looks like your hair.

## Work out your own grey first

Look at your hair in daylight, or at a recent photo, and ask three things.

**How much is grey?** A scattering, about half, or nearly all?

**What's it mixed with?** Dark brown, mid-brown, dark blonde, black? That base colour shows through and sets the whole tone.

**Is it warm or cool?** Some grey is steely and bluish. Some is soft and creamy. Hold something pure white next to your hair. If your hair looks yellowish beside it, you're warm. If it looks blue-grey, you're cool.

## How brands name their greys

**Jon Renau** puts greys in the numbers 34 to 60, plus 92. Roughly, the higher the number, the lighter and whiter the shade. Blends list more than one number.

**Ellen Wille** uses names. You'll see shades like Salt Pepper Mix and Smoke Mix, where "Mix" tells you several tones are blended together.

**Raquel Welch and Gabor** use names and codes, with SS marking a darker, shaded root.

Whatever the brand, the product description usually spells out the mix in words, something like "light brown with 75% grey". That sentence is more useful than the name.

## Choosing your shade

- **A little grey:** look for your base colour with grey blended in, often called pepper and salt. Avoid anything described as mostly white.
- **About half:** a balanced mix. These are the most forgiving and suit most skin tones.
- **Mostly grey or white:** silver and white blends. Pick one with a touch of a darker tone underneath or at the nape, which stops it looking flat.

If you're torn, Macmillan's general advice holds: go slightly lighter, not darker.

## Rooted greys

A grey with a slightly darker root can look very natural, because it copies the way grey grows in lighter around the face and darker behind. If you've always had a strong hairline colour, try it. If your own grey is even all over, an unrooted blend will look more like you.

## Around the face

Hair tends to go grey at the temples and front first. So a wig that's a touch lighter round the face and deeper at the back matches what people are used to seeing on you. Some shades are built that way. In Jon Renau's codes, F means the darker colour is kept solid at the nape.

## Going grey on purpose

Some women use a wig to stop colouring and skip the awkward growing-out months. If that's you, choose the grey you expect to end up with, based on your roots, and let your own hair catch up underneath.

## Check it in daylight

Grey shifts more than any other colour between a screen and real life, and between indoor light and a window. If the retailer lends colour rings or sells swatches, use them. See [how to choose a wig colour](/blogs/guides/how-to-choose-a-wig-colour) for how.
