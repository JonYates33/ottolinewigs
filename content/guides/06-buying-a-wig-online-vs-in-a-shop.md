There isn't a right answer to this one. Some women wouldn't dream of buying without trying on. Others would rather do the whole thing from the sofa with nobody watching. Here's what each is like.

## Buying in a shop or salon

**What's good about it**

- You try before you buy. Colour, size and how it sits on your face are all settled in one visit.
- Someone who fits wigs all day can spot what suits you faster than you can.
- Many will trim the wig for you there and then.

**What's less good**

- Fewer styles and colours than you'll find online, and often higher prices.
- You may have to travel, and appointments can be booked up.
- It can feel exposed. Macmillan acknowledges a fitting can be emotional, and says most fitters have a private room. You're entitled to ask for one.

**What happens at a fitting**

You'll usually talk through why you want a wig and what your own hair is like, then try several. Cancer Research UK suggests taking photos of your hair as you like it, and a friend or relative if you can. Macmillan adds that there's no pressure to decide on the day.

If you're having cancer treatment, ask your hospital first. Many arrange fittings themselves, and inpatients can ask their nurse for a fitter to come to the ward.

## Buying online

**What's good about it**

- Far more choice, and it's easy to compare prices.
- Private, and in your own time.
- Usually cheaper for the same wig.

**What's less good**

- Colour is a guess from a screen. This is the big one.
- You can't feel the cap or check the fit until it arrives.
- Returns on wigs are stricter than on clothes, for hygiene reasons.

## Seven checks before you order online

1. **Read the returns policy first.** Can you return a wig you've tried on? Within how many days? Most insist it's unworn, unwashed and still has its tags and hairnet.
2. **Look for help with colour.** Some retailers lend colour rings or sell swatches so you can see the shade in daylight. For a first wig that's well worth doing.
3. **Check it's the real brand.** The well-known brands are sold through approved stockists. Prices far below everyone else's are a warning sign.
4. **See how they handle VAT relief.** If you qualify, the form has to be done when you buy, so check the retailer offers it before you get to the checkout. See [VAT relief on wigs](/blogs/guides/vat-relief-on-wigs).
5. **Find out where it ships from.** Many UK retailers send wigs straight from the brand's warehouse. That's fine, but it affects delivery times.
6. **Make sure you can reach a person.** An email address or phone number with real replies matters when you've a question about fit.
7. **Know your size.** [Measure first](/blogs/guides/how-to-measure-your-head-for-a-wig).

## The best of both

Plenty of women mix the two:

- **Try on in person, then reorder online.** Once you know a style and colour suit you, buying the same one again online is low risk.
- **Buy online, then have it cut in.** Macmillan mentions that hairdressers trained in wigs can cut and style them, and that My New Hair lists salons that do.

## Which is right for a first wig?

If you can get to a fitting and you'd find it reassuring, go. Nothing matches seeing it on your own head.

If you can't, or you'd rather not, online is perfectly sound. Choose a retailer with a fair returns policy, sort out the colour before you commit, and plan on a trim.
